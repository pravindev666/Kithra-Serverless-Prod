# KITHRA — CUSTOMER DISCOVERY INTERVIEW GUIDE
**Framework:** *The Mom Test* by Rob Fitzpatrick  
**Target Market:** Urban Indian Adults (Ages 21–40; Bengaluru, Chennai, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Pune)  
**Status:** Ready-to-Execute Field Interview Instrument  
**Golden Rule:** *Never pitch Kithra during customer discovery. Talk about their real life, their actual past actions, and where they spent time or money.*

---

## 1. THE 3 CORE RULES OF THE MOM TEST

When interviewing potential users, people naturally want to be polite. If you say: *"Hey, I'm building this awesome psychology-based app that matches people on deep values instead of caste or photos—would you use it?"*, everyone will smile and say: *"Yes! That sounds incredible! I would definitely download it!"*

**That compliment is totally worthless.** It gives you false hope and leads you to spend 6 months building something nobody will actually open.

To extract the truth, follow these 3 non-negotiable rules:
1. **Rule 1: Talk about their life, not your idea.** (Do not mention Kithra, do not mention your algorithms, do not mention your brand).
2. **Rule 2: Ask about specific things they did in the past, not what they think they would do in the future.** (People are terrible at predicting their future behavior, but they cannot fake what they did last Tuesday).
3. **Rule 3: Talk less and listen more.** (If you are talking more than 20% of the time, you are pitching, not learning).

## 2. THE "WHAT NOT TO SAY" CHEAT SHEET

| Dangerous Leading Question (DO NOT ASK) | Why It Is Dangerous | The Mom Test Question (ASK THIS INSTEAD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"Do you think existing dating apps are shallow?"* | Leading question. Everyone will agree just to sound enlightened. | *"Tell me about the last time you went on a date or met someone through an app. How did it go?"* |
| *"Would you like an app that matches you on psychology?"* | Hypothetical future question. Costs nothing to say yes. | *"When you met the last person you dated, how did you figure out if you two were compatible?"* |
| *"Would you answer 10 questions to find a soulmate?"* | Imaginary commitment with an exaggerated promise. | *"Have you ever taken a personality test or filled out a questionnaire on an app? Can you walk me through the last one?"* |
| *"Would you pay ₹1,299 per month for meaningful connections?"* | Pure fiction. People always say yes until you put a credit card reader in front of them. | *"What was the last dating, matrimony, or social product you actually spent your own money on? What did you buy?"* |
| *"Don't you hate how matrimonial sites care about caste?"* | Pre-biases the response and triggers performative morality. | *"How have you and your family been approaching marriage searches recently? Can you walk me through a recent conversation?"* |

## 3. AUDIENCE-SPECIFIC INTERVIEW SCRIPTS

### SEGMENT 1: Adults Actively Seeking Long-Term Relationships or Matrimony
*Target Participant:* 25–35 year-old working professional in urban India thinking seriously about marriage.

#### Opening & Context (3 Mins)
> *"Hi [Name], thanks for taking 20 minutes to chat. I'm doing research on how people in India navigate finding long-term partners today. I'm not selling anything or pitching any product—I just want to understand what the real process looks like from your perspective."*

#### Core Behavioral Questions
1. *"Can you walk me through how you've been looking for a life partner over the past 6 months?"*
   * *Probe:* Did you use an app, a family connection, friends, or a matrimonial site?
2. *"Tell me about the last person you met or spoke with for marriage/long-term partnership. How did that introduction come about?"*
   * *Probe:* What was the very first piece of information you received about them?
3. *"When you first talked or met, what were you trying to figure out about them?"*
   * *Probe:* How did you figure that out? What questions did you ask?
4. *"Can you describe a specific time recently when a conversation or meeting did not work out? What happened?"*
   * *Probe:* At what point did you realize it wouldn't work? Was it before or after meeting?
5. *"What was the most frustrating part of that entire experience?"*
   * *Probe:* What did you do next? Did you change your search criteria or take a break?

---

### SEGMENT 2: Adults Currently Using Dating Apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge)
*Target Participant:* 21–32 year-old who has opened a dating app in the past 14 days.

#### Core Behavioral Questions
1. *"Can you pull out your phone and show me which apps you currently have installed? When was the last time you opened one?"*
   * *Probe:* What prompted you to open it that specific time? (Boredom, loneliness, notification?)
2. *"Tell me about the last profile you swiped right on (or liked). What specifically made you pause on that profile?"*
   * *Probe:* Did you read their bio, or look at the photos first?
3. *"Tell me about the last conversation you had on an app that actually led to an in-person meetup or phone call."*
   * *Probe:* How long did you chat before moving off the app? Who suggested moving off?
4. *"Now tell me about the last conversation that went nowhere or fizzled out. What happened?"*
   * *Probe:* Why did you stop replying (or why did they)?
5. *"Have you ever taken an in-app quiz or prompt (like Hinge voice prompts)? Did that change how you interacted with them?"*

---

### SEGMENT 3: Adults Seeking Platonic Friendship & Community
*Target Participant:* 22–35 year-old who recently moved to a new city (e.g. moved to Bengaluru for work) or wants closer friends.

#### Core Behavioral Questions
1. *"When was the last time you made a genuine new friend outside of work or college? How did you meet?"*
   * *Probe:* What made that connection stick compared to people you just met casually?
2. *"Have you ever tried using an app or community group (like Bumble BFF, Meetup, WhatsApp groups, board game clubs) to make friends?"*
   * *Probe:* Walk me through what happened when you tried it.
3. *"What felt awkward or unnatural about that process?"*
   * *Probe:* Did you ever meet in person? Why or why not?
4. *"If you wanted to find someone to hang out with this coming Saturday, what would you actually do right now?"*

---

### SEGMENT 4: Churned / Disillusioned Users Who Deleted Dating/Matrimony Apps
*Target Participant:* Adult who used an app for 3+ months, then uninstalled it in the last 6 months.

#### Core Behavioral Questions
1. *"Can you tell me about the day you decided to delete the app? What was the final straw?"*
   * *Probe:* What specific interaction or experience triggered that decision?
2. *"How long had you been using it before deleting it?"*
   * *Probe:* Did you meet anyone during that time? How many people?
3. *"After deleting it, how did your search for companionship change?"*
   * *Probe:* Did you replace it with something else, or just stop looking entirely?
4. *"Have you ever reinstalled it since then? What made you reinstall it?"*

---

### SEGMENT 5: Paying Customers (People Who Paid for Dating or Matrimony)
*Target Participant:* Adult who has spent money on Tinder Gold, Bumble Boost, HingeX, Shaadi.com VIP, or an offline matchmaker in the last 12 months.

#### Core Behavioral Questions
1. *"Can you tell me about the moment you pulled out your credit card or UPI to pay for that service?"*
   * *Probe:* What exact screen or paywall were you staring at when you decided to buy?
2. *"What specific outcome were you hoping to get from that purchase?"*
   * *Probe:* Was it seeing who liked you? Getting more matches? Sending a direct note?
3. *"After paying, did you actually get the outcome you paid for?"*
   * *Probe:* Did you renew the subscription when the month ended? Why or why not?
4. *"Looking back, did that purchase feel worth the money you spent?"*
   * *Probe:* What would have made it genuinely worth every rupee?

---

## 4. ESSENTIAL PROBING FOLLOW-UPS

Whenever a participant gives a vague answer (e.g., *"It's hard to find compatible people"*), **never** accept the generalization. Immediately dig for the specific story using these 5 neutral follow-ups:
- *"When was the last time that happened to you?"*
- *"Can you walk me through what you did next?"*
- *"Why was that a problem for you?"*
- *"What did you try to do to fix that?"*
- *"How much time or money did that cost you?"*
